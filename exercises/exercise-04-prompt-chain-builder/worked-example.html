Worked feedback and routes

Local simulation · No AI service connected · Nothing is sent or saved

A defensible route

Map: extraction → risk analysis → test ideas. Use current/separate/linked/grounding at Stage 1; handoff/impact/linked/grounding at Stage 2; handoff/oracles/linked/grounding at Stage 3. At each gate, cite a source and explicitly inspect what the response did and did not establish. Passing this route yields a small proposal that includes fresh-session new- and old-password checks (AC-04) and used-link replay (AC-03). A broader test proposal could also cover AC-02 before/after expiry; the three-idea limit and credential-protection goal explain this route's focus, not exclusive correctness.

Possible Stage 1 note: “AC-04 supports both credential outcomes. Policy length and the exact 30-minute boundary remain open.” A reasonable Stage 2 note: “AC-03/04 support the impact concerns; likelihood is unknown.” A reasonable Stage 3 note: “AC-04 needs both fresh-session outcomes; AC-03 replay is a separate oracle.” These notes illustrate review and are not machine-scored answers.

An imperfect route and downstream effect

At Stage 1, using the suggestion or complete-the-policy block yields “Enforce a 12-character minimum (AC-04).” That citation is invalid: AC-04 says nothing about length. If the learner passes it anyway, Stage 2 receives that exact text. A risk shortcut can elevate it into a “confirmed policy risk”; Stage 3 can then create an unsupported rejection oracle. The appropriate review is to hold, revise and rerun, or carry it forward only while explicitly flagging it for clarification. The simulation never repairs it silently.

A different weak route omits the old-password outcome or treats confirmation as enough. Even a polished response then misses the AC-04 credential oracle. An unreferenced output makes review harder but does not automatically make every statement false. Unknown likelihood cannot be turned into measured likelihood from the given sources.

Revision and debrief

After an initial full route, change the Stage 1 input/task block or review a different response. Preserve the initial route. The Stage 2 input is now different, so Stage 2 and Stage 3 are “Needs review”. Rerun or reaffirm Stage 2 against the new exact text; only then can a revised Stage 3 be current. A two-minute revision may reasonably stop after that first downstream review.

Ask: What did the next prompt actually receive? Which claim should have been stopped or corrected? What changed when the earlier prompt or review gate changed?

These examples are teaching feedback, not proof of a real system's behaviour, a model's quality, or a learner's understanding.

From Prompts to Agentic Testing Workflows

Other task orders

Every order is allowed. Starting with risks or tests uses the current source cards. A later task receives the exact reviewed previous response, while source cards remain available for checking. Extraction after risk or test work reveals a dependency that arrived late; it cannot retroactively correct the earlier decision. Discuss what the alternative order helped or obscured.

Take-home continuation

After the live three-stage chain, a longer workflow can ask for priority trade-offs, automation suitability and setup dependencies, then expert review. Each new stage should receive a reviewed handoff and retain open questions. These are later design tasks, not requirements for this timed activity.

Compare the two chains

For the live checkpoint, compare the first and revised responses at the first affected downstream task. A revised response that promotes the 12-character suggestion into confirmed policy is weaker because AC-04 does not establish that rule; a handoff that flags it for clarification may be more defensible. Source IDs alone do not settle the comparison. If a later stage still says “Needs review”, do not call the revised final proposal complete.

At home, rerun and review the remaining downstream task, then compare the two final outputs for source support, both AC-04 credential outcomes, useful oracles, and stated limits. “First”, “revised”, “neither ready”, or “no clear advantage” can be justified from the actual two responses; the simulator does not assign a hidden score.
